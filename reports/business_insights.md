## 7. Opportunities and recommendations

| # | Recommendation | Supporting metric | Estimate and its assumption |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Set a discount ceiling (for example 30%) and require approval above it | 984 lines (11.4%) were discounted over 30% and lost USD 190,989 (Q13a) | Capping those lines at 30% would add about USD 142,142 of profit **if the same units still sold**. Treat as an upper bound: volume will likely fall |
| 2 | Review pricing and discount policy for Tables, Bookcases, Machines | Net loss USD 70,422; 44% to 62% of their sale lines lose money (Q09b) | Even undiscounted, thin margins leave little room to discount; a cost review may be needed (the data has no cost field to confirm) |
| 3 | Investigate discounting in the Central region | Margin 11.6% vs 20.5% elsewhere; 53% of lines discounted (Q10a) | Closing the margin gap would be worth about USD 64,455 over the period, **assuming the region's product mix matches the others** |
| 4 | Report profit, not only revenue, in product rankings | 14 top-quartile revenue products earn under 10% margin | No estimate: this is a reporting change |
| 5 | Plan inventory and staffing for Q4 | Nov-Dec carry 26.9% of annual revenue (Q05a) | No estimate |
| 6 | Fix data entry controls for price and ship date | 6 suspected price keying errors, 14 invalid ship dates, 23 invalid rows quarantined (data quality report) | The flagged price lines hold 0.6% of revenue and 3.3% of profit that may be overstated |


**How to read the estimates.** They are simple what-if calculations on this dataset, with the assumption stated beside each. They are not forecasts.

## Limitations
- Synthetic data: no real-world conclusions.
- No cost, inventory or returns-reason fields, so the causes of low margin cannot be separated into price versus cost.
- One country, four years, one currency.
- Customer 'new vs returning' is censored at the start of the data window.
